This is a piecewise function: $$ p_1(t) = \begin{cases} 1 + t, & t \in [-1, 0] \\ 1 - t, & t \in [0, 1] \end{cases} $$ The CDF $P_1(t)$ is the integral of the PDF, $\int_{-1}^{t} p_1(s) ds$. For $t \in [-1, 0]$: $$ P_1(t) = \int_{-1}^{t} (1 + s) ds = \left[s + \frac{s^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{t} = \left(t + \frac{t^2}{2}\right) - \left(-1 + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{t^2}{2} + t + \frac{1}{2} $$ For $t \in [0, 1]$: $$ P_1(t) = P_1(0) + \int_{0}^{t} (1-s)ds = \frac{1}{2} + \left[s - \frac{s^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{t} = -\frac{t^2}{2} + t + \frac{1}{2} $$ To find the inverse, we set $u = P_1(t)$ and solve for $t$. For $u \in [0, 0.5]$, corresponding to $t \in [-1, 0]$: $$ u = \frac{t^2}{2} + t + \frac{1}{2} \implies t^2 + 2t + (1 - 2u) = 0 $$ The quadratic formula gives $t = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 4(1 - 2u)}}{2} = -1 \pm \sqrt{2u}$. To keep $t$ in the range $[-1, 0]$, we must choose the positive root, so $t = \sqrt{2u} - 1$. For $u \in (0.5, 1]$, corresponding to $t \in [0, 1]$: $$ u = -\frac{t^2}{2} + t + \frac{1}{2} \implies t^2 - 2t + (2u - 1) = 0 $$ The quadratic formula gives $t = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 4(2u - 1)}}{2} = 1 \pm \sqrt{2 - 2u}$. To keep $t$ in the range $[0, 1]$, we must choose the negative root, so $t = 1 - \sqrt{2 - 2u}$.
