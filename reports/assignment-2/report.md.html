This is a piecewise function: $$ p_1(t) = \begin{cases} 1 + t, & t \in [-1, 0] \\ 1 - t, & t \in [0, 1] \end{cases} $$ The CDF $P_1(t)$ is the integral of the PDF, $\int_{-1}^{t} p_1(s) ds$. For $t \in [-1, 0]$: $$ P_1(t) = \int_{-1}^{t} (1 + s) ds = \left[s + \frac{s^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{t} = \left(t + \frac{t^2}{2}\right) - \left(-1 + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{t^2}{2} + t + \frac{1}{2} $$ For $t \in [0, 1]$: $$ P_1(t) = P_1(0) + \int_{0}^{t} (1-s)ds = \frac{1}{2} + \left[s - \frac{s^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{t} = -\frac{t^2}{2} + t + \frac{1}{2} $$ To find the inverse, set $u = P_1(t)$ and solve for $t$. For $u \in [0, 0.5]$, corresponding to $t \in [-1, 0]$: $$ u = \frac{t^2}{2} + t + \frac{1}{2} \implies t^2 + 2t + (1 - 2u) = 0 $$ The quadratic formula gives $t = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 4(1 - 2u)}}{2} = -1 \pm \sqrt{2u}$. To keep $t$ in the range $[-1, 0]$, we must choose the positive root, so $t = \sqrt{2u} - 1$. For $u \in (0.5, 1]$, corresponding to $t \in [0, 1]$: $$ u = -\frac{t^2}{2} + t + \frac{1}{2} \implies t^2 - 2t + (2u - 1) = 0 $$ The quadratic formula gives $t = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 4(2u - 1)}}{2} = 1 \pm \sqrt{2 - 2u}$. To keep $t$ in the range $[0, 1]$, we must choose the negative root, so $t = 1 - \sqrt{2 - 2u}$. The final inverse function is: $$ t = P_1^{-1}(u) = \begin{cases} \sqrt{2u} - 1, & u \in [0, 0.5] \\ 1 - \sqrt{2 - 2u}, & u \in (0.5, 1] \end{cases} $$ ### Tent visualize_warping_ui() Output ![Tent Distribution](images/tent.png) ## Uniform Disk For a uniform distribution on a unit disk with radius $R = 1$ and area $A = $\pi R^2 = \pi$, the PDF is constant, $p(x,y) = 1/A = 1/\pi$. The angle $\theta$ is uniformly distributed, so the mapping is direct: $$ \theta = 2\pi v $$ The probability density along the radius is not uniform. It is proportional to the radius, $p(r) \propto r$. Normalizing this PDF over $r \in [0, 1]$ gives $p(r) = 2r$. The probability density function (PDF) for the radius, $p(r)$, must be normalized so its integral over the domain $[0, 1]$ equals 1. We have $p(r) = c \cdot r$, where $c$ is the normalization constant. To find $c$: $$ \int_{0}^{1} c \cdot r \,dr = 1 $$ $$ = c \int_{0}^{1} r \,dr = c \left[ \frac{r^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{1} = c \left( \frac{1^2}{2} - \frac{0^2}{2} \right) = c \left( \frac{1}{2} \right) = 1 $$ $$ \implies c = 2 $$ So the PDF is: $$ p(r) = 2r $$ The CDF for the radius is: $$ P(r) = \int_{0}^{r} 2s\,ds = s^2 \Big|_0^r = r^2 $$ To invert the CDF, we set a uniform sample $u = P(r)$ and solve for $r$: $$ u = r^2 \implies r = \sqrt{u} $$ Combining these and converting to Cartesian coordinates gives the final mapping: $$ x = r \cos(\theta) = \sqrt{u} \cos(2\pi v) $$ $$ y = r \sin(\theta) = \sqrt{u} \sin(2\pi v) $$ ### Uniform Disk visualize_warping_ui() Output ![Uniform Disk Distribution](images/uniform_disk.png) ## Uniform Sphere For a uniform distribution on the surface of a unit sphere with radius $R = 1$ and area $A = 4\pi R^2 = 4\pi$, the PDF with respect to surface area is constant, $p(point) = 1/A = 1/(4\pi)$. The surface area of a differential band on the sphere is proportional to $\sin\theta$, so the PDF for $\theta$ is $p(\theta) \propto \sin\theta$. The PDF $p(\theta)$ must be normalized so its integral over the domain $[0, \pi]$ equals 1. Starting with $p(\theta) = c \cdot \sin\theta$ and solving for the constant $c$: $$ \int_{0}^{\pi} c \cdot \sin\theta \,d\theta = c \left[ -\cos\theta \right]_{0}^{\pi} = c \left( -(-1) - (-1) \right) = 2c = 1 $$ $$ \implies c = \frac{1}{2} $$ So the normalized PDF is $p(\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\sin\theta$.
