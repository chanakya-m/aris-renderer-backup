Before the correct RHS is implemented, the neural network is being trained to predict the incoming radiance at a surface point based on that point's properties (position, normal, view direction, and albedo). The LHS is computed by taking the features of the first camera-ray intersection and feeding them into the network. NOTE Briefly describe how you compute RHS. Include the final lhs and rhs images. The RHS serves as the "teacher" signal for the neural network. It calculates a one-bounce path tracing estimate for the scattered light at the initial camera ray intersection points. After the direct emission `E` has been calculated, for every point `x` that did not hit an emitter, the `scene.sample_brdf()` function is called. This generates a new random ray direction `wi` based on the surface material and returns a `brdf_val` that incorporates the material's color, the cosine term, and the sampling PDF. A second ray is then traced into the scene, starting from the initial hit point `x` (with an offset along the surface normal) in the new direction `wi`. If this second ray intersects another surface at a point `x'`, the neural network itself is queried using the properties of this second hit point. The network's output, which is its prediction of the light leaving `x'`, is then retrieved. The final Monte Carlo estimate for the one-bounce reflected light is the product of the `brdf_val` from the first surface and the `network_output` from the second surface. This scattered light value is then added to `E` to form the complete `RHS`. ## Rendered images ![LHS 4000 steps](images/4000lhs.png) ![RHS 4000 steps](images/4000rhs.png) # Rendering Neural Radiosity !!!
